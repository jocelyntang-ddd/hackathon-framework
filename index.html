1

Map Your Workflow

Get concrete about what you actually do

30 min

Before you look for AI anything, pick one process the whole team knows cold and lay it out, start to finish.

  • Every step, in order — trigger to completion: "manager sends request" → "I pull data from Sheet X" → "I reformat it" → "I email it to Y"
  • Who's involved at each step
  • The tools used at each step — email, spreadsheets, forms, whatever it actually is
  • Roughly how long each step takes
Brainstorm on paper, whiteboard, or a shared Google doc. Don't try to hold it all in your head.
2

Hunt for Repetitive, Time-Consuming Tasks

Run every step through six tests

20 min

Go back through your map. Flag every step that hits 2 or more of these:

Repetition

Do I do this weekly, daily, or every time a certain event happens?

Copy-paste test

Am I moving the same info from one place to another?

Template test

Do I start from a blank page for what's really the same output?

Waiting test

Am I stuck waiting on someone else's manual step?

Dread test

Which step do I personally avoid or procrastinate on?

5-minute teach

Is it fully rule-based, explainable in 5 minutes?

Hits 2+ tests? It makes your shortlist.
3

Frame the Problem, Not the Solution

Write it down before you name a tool

15 min

For each flagged task, write a problem statement — never a tool name.

✕"We need a chatbot."
✓"Every week, the L&D coordinator spends ~2 hours manually copying survey responses into a summary report for leadership."

A good statement names who does it, how often, how long it takes, and what's painful about it.

Pick one problem statement for the hackathon.
4

Prioritize with an Impact/Effort Lens

Sanity-check against the judging criteria

10 min
How much time would this save per instance?
Feeds the "time saved" score
How often does this happen?
Feeds the "frequency" score
Who else does something similar?
Feeds the "scalability / reach" score
Can we realistically build this by Monday?
Feasibility — this one is a dealbreaker.
Low on feasibility? Find a smaller part of the same problem you can actually finish.
5

Choose Your Tool

Match the problem type to a tool category — then stop deliberating

don't overthink

Tap what your task involves. You don't need the "best" tool — you need one that works fast.

Writing, rewriting, summarizing, drafting
Chat with an AI model directly. Paste your raw content, describe the format you need, and iterate.
ClaudeChatGPTGemini
💡 Start with a real example from your workflow, not a generic prompt — it gets you to a usable draft faster.
Automating manual steps
Have the model write the automation for you: a Google Apps Script if you live in Sheets/Docs, or a Python script for anything more general.
Gemini for Apps ScriptChatGPTClaude for Python
💡 Describe the trigger and the desired end state — let the model figure out the steps in between.
Moving data between apps automatically
Sheets, email, Slack, forms — if data needs to hop between systems on its own, reach for a connector platform instead of custom code.
ZapierMake.comn8n
💡 These trade some flexibility for speed of setup — usually the right trade for a five-day hackathon.
A simple internal tool, dashboard, or tracker
Need an actual mini-app people click into? No-code builders get you a working version fastest.
LovableBase44
💡 Wireframe it on paper first — five minutes there saves an hour of rebuilding later.
Spreadsheet formulas, cleaning messy data
Formula generation, messy-to-clean transforms, or quick pivots — describe what the data looks like now and what you need it to look like.
ClaudeChatGPTGemini
💡 Paste a small sample of fake data so the model can see the actual shape of the mess.
Switching tools mid-hackathon costs more time than an imperfect first pick. Choose one and move.
Step 6 · No Time Limit
Prototype in Small Loops
Don't build the whole thing in one pass. Work in short cycles — each an hour or two, not a full day.

The loop, four times over

  • Build the smallest possible version — the dumbest thing that still saves time
  • Test it on fake data immediately, before it feels "done"
  • Iterate on whatever breaks or feels clunky
  • Repeat
Rule of thumb: by Friday evening, something should run end-to-end — rough is fine. Polish comes later.

Step 8 · Final Check
Prepare Your Submission
Revisit the submission requirements from the planning doc. Tap each one as you can answer it — the guide won't save your progress, so do this together as a team right before you submit.
What was the problem?Named clearly — who, how often, how painful.
What did you build?The smallest working version, described plainly.
What does it save?Time, effort, or errors — with real numbers from Step 7.
Who else could use it?And what would it take to roll this out more broadly.
